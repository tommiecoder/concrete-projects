The Strategy Layer Is Becoming the Product

Concrete’s latest integrations suggest an evolution from individual vaults toward a programmable allocation layer across specialized DeFi infrastructure.

Reporting period: September 24–30, 2026·Research timestamp: September 28, 2026·Reporting week in progress

FACT

Concrete lists a Morpho USD vault that automates USDC deposits across multiple Morpho vaults, and a Concrete × Morph vault set that supports nine assets and allocates to Morph-ecosystem protocols.

ANALYSIS

Taken together, these products place Concrete one layer above individual protocols. It selects, sizes and rebalances exposure to other venues instead of being the venue.

DATA BOUNDARY

No new event after September 28, 2026 is asserted. Allocation weights, live TVL per venue and several launch-asset details were not independently verified. They are labeled where they appear.

01 / Executive Summary

One layer above the opportunity

For most of DeFi’s history, a “vault” meant access to one strategy: deposit here, earn from that protocol. Concrete’s product architecture increasingly sits one layer above individual DeFi opportunities. Instead of exposing a depositor to a single protocol, a Concrete vault can hold capital across several strategies and underlying venues, and move it between them under a defined allocation process.

Two integrations show this most clearly. Concrete × Morpho is a Concrete vault whose underlying positions are themselves Morpho vaults run by independent curators, so it allocates across allocators. Concrete × Morph is a set of vaults on the Morph network that deploy deposits into multiple partner protocols in that ecosystem. In both cases the depositor holds one position, and the multi-venue work happens underneath it.

This issue presents that as an observable direction in Concrete’s product architecture, not as proof that Concrete is, or will become, a general allocator for DeFi. The architecture has advantages, and it also adds dependencies. Both are covered below.

The vault is a portfolio. An allocator, which Concrete’s documentation describes as an automated operational role, decides how capital is spread across approved venues and when to move it. The user keeps one share token while the underlying mix can change.

The difference in one sentence

In the older model, choosing a venue is the user’s job. In the newer model it becomes the vault’s job, which means the vault’s allocation logic, permissions and risk limits become part of what the depositor is exposed to.

03 / Concrete × Morpho

An allocator over allocators

Morpho provides lending infrastructure: isolated lending markets, and Morpho Vaults in which independent curators allocate across those markets. Concrete’s Morpho products add another layer that allocates across Morpho vaults.

Documented

What public sources state

  • Concrete’s Morpho USD Vault “automates USDC deposits across multiple Morpho vaults,” naming vaults operated by Gauntlet, MEV Capital, RE7 Labs and others.
  • Concrete’s Morpho Bitcoin Vault automates LBTC deposits across multiple Morpho vaults.
  • Concrete describes its system as allocating, rebalancing and compounding across on-chain opportunities, with the Allocator as an automated role.
Sources: app.concrete.xyz vault pages; Concrete docs · reviewed September 28, 2026.
Interpretation

Why it matters

Morpho curators already make market-level allocation decisions. A Concrete vault on top turns curator selection into a programmable decision. It chooses which curated vaults to hold and rebalances among them. That is a portfolio-construction layer, not a new lending market.

The trade-off is that depositors take on Concrete’s logic, each curator’s logic and Morpho’s contracts together. Diversifying across curators does not diversify away shared market exposure if those curators lend against similar collateral.

What not to assume

Nine supported assets does not mean nine copies of one strategy. A BGB vault, a stablecoin vault and a restaked-ETH vault face different venues, liquidity depth and risk. Concrete also groups some assets, as in a “Majors” vault for ETH, WBTC, USDT and USDC. Per-asset strategy composition was not verified for this issue.

The Morph integration dates from early 2025, which predates this reporting week. It appears here as architectural evidence, not as news.

06 / Why This Architecture Matters

Four potential advantages, five costs

01 / ABSTRACTION

One position, many venues

The depositor manages one share token instead of several protocol positions, approvals and dashboards.

02 / REBALANCING

Movement without exits

Capital can shift between venues inside the vault without each depositor withdrawing and redepositing.

03 / DIVERSIFICATION

Strategy breadth

Exposure can span several venues and curators, which may reduce dependence on any single one.

04 / COMPOSABILITY

Shares as building blocks

ct[asset] shares are ERC-20 tokens, so other protocols can integrate them where they choose to.

Tradeoffs: these remain

More contract dependencies

Each layer (vault, allocator, curator vault, protocol) is code that can fail.

Allocator / curator risk

Allocation decisions can be wrong, slow or poorly parameterized.

Underlying protocol risk

A vault is no safer than the venues it allocates to.

Hidden complexity

A simple interface can obscure how many moving parts sit behind it.

Liquidity coordination

Withdrawals depend on unwinding positions across several venues.

Reading rule

The architecture redistributes risk. It does not eliminate it. Advantages 1–4 are potential benefits whose value depends on execution, and the five costs apply however well the system is run.

Why an allocation layer could matter to institutions

These are the properties institutional allocators usually look for. They are not claims that institutions are using these specific vaults, and no such adoption was verified for this issue.

STANDARDIZED ACCESS

One interface, many venues

A single ERC-4626-style share replaces separate integrations with each protocol.

RISK CONTROLS

Bounded allocation

Concrete documents role separation, bounded accounting, monitoring and pause authority. Whether these meet a given mandate requires diligence.

PORTFOLIO ABSTRACTION

Positions as a portfolio

Exposure is expressed as one holding with an underlying mix, which is closer to how funds are reported.

STRATEGY SELECTION

Choosing the chooser

The decision moves from picking markets to picking an allocation framework and its constraints.

REPORTING

NAV-based accounting

Share value tied to vault NAV fits fund-style reporting, subject to the transparency of underlying positions.

OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY

Fewer manual moves

Rebalancing inside a vault can reduce transactions, approvals and monitoring load for the holder.

10 / What Could Break?

Stress-testing the thesis

An allocation layer is only as strong as the weakest thing it allocates to and the logic that decides where to allocate. These five questions are the ones worth asking of any multi-venue vault.

01

What happens if an underlying protocol fails?

Losses in one venue flow through to vault NAV in proportion to exposure. An allocator can limit position sizes, but it cannot reverse a loss that has already happened. The share token carries the loss to every holder.

Diligence: per-venue caps, exposure disclosure, loss-socialization rules.

02

What happens if liquidity disappears?

Withdrawals rely on unwinding positions. If lending utilization spikes or pools thin out, redemptions may queue. Some Concrete vaults already document withdrawal queues or lockups.

Diligence: queue mechanics, liquid buffer size, worst-case exit timing.

03

What if two “diversified” strategies have the same exposure?

Three curated vaults can all lend against the same collateral or depend on the same oracle or stablecoin. Diversification by venue name is not diversification by risk factor.

Diligence: look-through collateral, oracle and asset exposure across venues.

04

What if the allocator reacts poorly to a regime change?

Models tuned on calm markets can chase yield into crowded venues, rebalance too late, or churn and pay costs. Automation executes the model faithfully, including its blind spots.

Diligence: allocation constraints, human override, historical behavior in stress.

05

What happens when multiple layers of smart contracts interact?

Composability risk grows with depth. An upgrade, pause or accounting edge case at one layer can behave unexpectedly at another. Audits are typically scoped per contract, not per stack.

Diligence: audit scope across integrations, upgrade authority, pause propagation.

12 / Conclusion
The vault may be the visible product. The allocation layer may be the deeper infrastructure.

Concrete’s Morpho and Morph products show a vault design in which the core work is choosing, sizing and moving exposure across other venues, not providing a single yield source. If that direction continues, Concrete’s longer-term differentiation may depend on how effectively it coordinates capital across an expanding set of on-chain strategies while keeping risk, liquidity and transparency manageable for the people holding the share token.

That is a framework for evaluation, not a forecast. Each additional venue adds opportunity and dependency at the same time. The questions in Section 10 will matter more than the number of integrations.
